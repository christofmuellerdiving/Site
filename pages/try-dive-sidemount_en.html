My focus

Afterwards you know whether sidemount suits you and what kit you need, before you spend any money.

On a try dive you take sidemount or doubles for a test dive. You need an Open Water certification or equivalent. Equipment is included in the price, and the try dive takes at least half a day.

How it works

We start on land. I show you how the cylinders hang, how the hoses are routed and where the weight goes, and set the system up for you. Before the dive we practise the S-drill on land, where you donate gas to your buddy.

Then we dive. I watch your trim and how you handle the cylinders. After the dive you tell me how it felt, and I tell you what I saw.

Before you buy

Many problems on the first sidemount dives go away with practice, no new gear needed. I can see underwater whether that applies to you. Then you decide whether to buy anything, and what.

Who it is for

The try dive fits if you are already certified and thinking about a sidemount course or a move to doubles. Like all my courses, it takes place in a lake.

Where to go next

If sidemount suits you, the next step is SDI Sidemount Diver. If you are heading for technical diving, you learn the foundations in TDI Intro to Tech, which I also teach in sidemount. For doubles, the way forward is the Doubles Primer or GUE Fundamentals.

We sort out what you bring yourself in a short phone call before the date.
