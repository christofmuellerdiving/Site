TDI Intro to Tech

The foundations of technical diving without decompression, taught in sidemount. Can be combined with Advanced Nitrox.

My focus

We do the theory at a table, talking it through. That is where we work out gas planning and go through failures before we practise them in the water.

Intro to Tech is the step into technical diving. You get to know technical configurations, work on buoyancy, trim and situational awareness, and plan your gas the way technical divers do. All dives stay within no-decompression limits. You may use nitrox as far as your certification allows. I teach the course in sidemount.

The three days

We meet each day at 8:30 at Deepstop in Schwetzingen. The day starts with theory in the training room, then we set up equipment and walk through the drills on land. After that we do one or two dives in a lake nearby, and the day ends with a debrief. The standard requires at least six hours of classroom and briefings and at least three dives, none deeper than 23 m.

Theory

From physics we review pressure. Physiology covers ascent and descent rates, hyperthermia, hypothermia and stress. On equipment we look at cylinders and valves, regulators, sidemount system, instruments, spools, lift bags and exposure protection, along with taking only what you need and stowing it so nothing dangles. In planning you calculate your SAC rate and the minimum gas reserve for no-decompression dives. You also decide how the team enters and exits, descends and ascends, and what happens if a gas supply fails.

On land and in the water

On land you prepare your equipment, match gas within the team and set limits based on your own gas consumption and the team’s. Before every dive we run START: S-drill with gas donation and bubble check, team check, gas matching, route and the key figures of the plan.

In the water we check your weighting first. Then you hover in one spot without moving hands or feet and stay horizontal on the descent, on the bottom and on the ascent. You learn four kicks that don’t stir up silt: frog kick, modified frog kick, flutter kick and back kick. Regulator exchange and recovery, flooding, removing and clearing your mask, the S-drill and deploying a surface marker buoy all happen mid-water, without losing trim or buoyancy.

Afterwards

With the certification you may dive in a technical configuration without direct supervision, as long as the dives and conditions match those of the course. From there you can go on to TDI Advanced Nitrox and Decompression Procedures. If you combine the course with Advanced Nitrox, the standard requires four dives for both together.

Who it is for

You need SDI Open Water Scuba Diver or equivalent and 25 logged open water dives, and you must be at least 18, or 15 with parental consent. The course fits if you want to start technical diving and choose sidemount to do it. If you want to start in backmount, my course for that is GUE Fundamentals. How the two courses differ is on the blog: GUE Fundamentals or TDI Intro to Tech?
